Introduction : With compound interest, each year's interest is added to the principal and earns interest in turn. This is the mechanism behind long-term saving. This sheet gives the formula, compares it with simple interest and covers changing the period.

## Compounding
### Interest that earns interest
At the end of each year, the interest is added to the principal. The following year, interest is calculated on this new principal. So you multiply by the same factor every period.

::: definition Compounding
An operation that adds interest to the principal at the end of each period, so that it earns interest itself.
:::

::: formule
Cₙ = C₀ × (1 + i)ⁿ
:::

::: exemple €1,000 at 10 % for 3 years
- Year 1: 1,000 × 1.10 = €1,100.
- Year 2: 1,100 × 1.10 = €1,210.
- Year 3: 1,210 × 1.10 = €1,331.
- That is 1,000 × 1.10³ = €1,331.
:::

## Compound versus simple
### A gap that widens
For n = 1, both methods give the same result. As soon as n > 1 and i > 0, (1 + i)ⁿ exceeds 1 + i × n. The gap grows with time.

::: courbe €1,000 invested at 5 % a year
Axe : 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10
Simple interest | 1 000 | 1 100 | 1 200 | 1 300 | 1 400 | 1 500
Compound interest | 1 000 | 1 102,50 | 1 215,51 | 1 340,10 | 1 477,46 | 1 628,89
Unité : €
Note : the axis shows the term in years. After 10 years, the gap is €128.89.
:::

::: attention The rate is not multiplied by the term
With compound interest, 3 years at 10 % do not give 30 % interest, but 1.10³ − 1 = 33.1 %.
:::

## Equivalent rates
### Changing period without making mistakes
Two rates are equivalent if they give the same accumulated value over one year. To go from a half-yearly rate to an annual rate, you square the factor.

::: formule
1 + annual i = (1 + half-yearly i)²
:::

::: exemple A half-yearly rate of 10 %
(1 + 0.10)² = 1.21, or 21 % a year. It is not 20 %: the first half-year's interest earns interest in the second.
:::

## À retenir
- Accumulated value with compound interest: Cₙ = C₀ × (1 + i)ⁿ.
- You multiply n times by the same factor 1 + i.
- For n = 1, compound and simple are equal.
- For n > 1 and i > 0, compound interest gives more than simple interest.
- The gap between the two methods widens with time.
- Equivalent rates: you compound the factors, you don't add the rates.
- Interest is the accumulated value minus the principal.
