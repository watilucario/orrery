Introduction : Remembering is built by retrieving, not by rereading. This chapter explains active recall, the role of your confidence and the place of mistakes. That is why the app asks you to answer before showing the correction.

## Active recall
### Retrieve rather than reread
::: definition Active recall
Trying to **retrieve information from memory**, without looking at it, then checking.
:::

Roediger and Karpicke (2006) describe the testing effect: testing yourself helps you remember longer than rereading. Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) rank rereading and highlighting among the least useful techniques.

::: attention Rereading is not reviewing
A text you have reread feels familiar. But recognizing is not retrieving. On exam day, nobody hands you the page.
:::

::: etapes A session of active recall
Question | Read it without looking at the answer
Attempt | Answer from memory, even if you hesitate
Confidence | Say whether you are sure or not
Correction | Compare with the right answer
Scheduling | The app sets the next date
:::

## Metacognition and confidence
### Knowing what you know
::: definition Metacognition
The ability to **judge your own knowledge**: knowing what you know, and what you do not.
:::

After several rereadings, you may believe you know: this is the illusion of knowing. A test reveals it. Giving your confidence before the correction helps you calibrate better.

| Result | Confidence | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Right | Sure | Solid knowledge |
| Right | Unsure | Fragile memory |
| Wrong | Sure | False certainty |
| Wrong | Unsure | Known gap |

## Mistakes
### A mistake is information
A test also serves to spot what you do not know. A mistake corrected right away helps you remember the right answer. The hypercorrection effect (Butterfield and Metcalfe, 2001) is the finding that a mistake made with confidence is often corrected better.

::: exemple Two mistakes, two next steps
You answer "80 %" with doubt, the right answer is 90 %: you note that the idea is fragile.
You answer "80 %" while sure: you have a false certainty. Take the time to read the explanation.
:::

## À retenir
- Active recall means retrieving from memory, then checking.
- The testing effect: testing yourself helps you remember better than rereading.
- Rereading gives familiarity, not a memory that lasts.
- Metacognition is the judgment you make about your own knowledge.
- The illusion of knowing is revealed by a test.
- Give your confidence before the correction.
- A confident mistake is a false certainty to fix quickly.
- Getting things wrong in a session is useful, not a failure.

Sources : Roediger and Karpicke, Test-enhanced learning (2006) ; Dunlosky et al. (2013) ; Butterfield and Metcalfe (2001) ; how the daily session works in the app.
